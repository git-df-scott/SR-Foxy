# Branched double covers of the Abe–Tagami family, and a sharp d-invariant gate

14 September 2026. **No counterexample found.** This note supplies data the
repository repeatedly recorded as missing. `RESUMED_2026-09-12.md` states
plainly that "branched-cover and satellite data are not covered", and lists
"use additional geometric or branched-cover information" as the next
falsification step after the involutive local-equivalence collapse. Nothing
below is a slice disk, and nothing below obstructs anything yet.

## 1. Construction, and why the obvious one is wrong

Build Σ₂(K) by Dehn filling the exterior along (2,0) — the orbifold S³ with
cone angle π along K — and then taking the 2-fold cyclic cover, letting the
covering carry the induced filling. The induced slope comes back as (1,0) on
the single cusp of the cover, as it must.

Taking the cyclic cover **first** and then guessing a filling slope does not
work, and the failure is silent. H₁ of the 2-fold cyclic cover of the exterior
is Z/13 ⊕ Z, the free summand generated by the lift of μ², and the lift of λ
is torsion. So every slope μ̃ + kλ̃ kills the free summand and returns Z/13:
homology cannot distinguish the true branched-cover slope from the 17 other
short slopes that also return Z/13. This was checked directly; see
`scripts/branched_double_cover_gate.py`.

**Controls.** The construction returns π₁ = ⟨a | a³⟩, ⟨a | a⁵⟩, ⟨a | a¹³⟩ for
3_1, 4_1, 6_3 — exactly L(3,1), L(5,q), L(13,q). For the granny knot
3_1 # 3_1 it returns H₁ = Z/3 ⊕ Z/3 with a rich family of low-index subgroups,
which is the positive control for detecting a non-cyclic fundamental group.
Every profile below was recomputed from an independently simplified diagram of
the same knot and agreed.

## 2. The separating invariant is combinatorial, not numerical

A lens space L(p,q) has cyclic π₁, so it has no subgroup of index d unless
d divides p. Low-index subgroup enumeration is exact. No hyperbolic structure,
volume, or isometry test is used, and none is needed — which matters here,
because SnapPy does not find a positively oriented solution for Σ₂(K₁) or
Σ₂(K₂) and `volume(verified=True)` fails on both.

| knot | H₁(Σ₂) | subgroups of index 2,3,4,5 (their H₁) |
|---|---|---|
| K₀ = 6_3 | Z/13 | none |
| K₁ = A₁(6_3) | Z/13 | three of index 5, each Z/3 ⊕ Z/117 |
| K₂ = A₂(6_3) | Z/13 | three of index 5: Z/6 ⊕ Z/78 (twice), Z/2 ⊕ Z/6 ⊕ Z/234 ⊕ Z |

Consequences, all rigorous:

1. **Σ₂(K₀) = L(13,5)**, since 6_3 is the two-bridge knot S(13,5); the
   computed π₁ = ⟨a | a¹³⟩ confirms it.
2. **Σ₂(K₁) is not a lens space**, so Σ₂(K₀) ≇ Σ₂(K₁). Since H₁ = Z/13 admits
   no surjection onto Z/5 or D₅, the index-5 subgroups force π₁(Σ₂(K₁)) to
   surject onto a non-solvable group.
3. **Σ₂(K₁) ≇ Σ₂(K₂)**, by the index-5 subgroup homologies.
4. One index-5 cover of Σ₂(K₂) has positive first Betti number.

This is the first invariant computed in this repository that separates members
of the Abe–Tagami family at all. It is **not** a concordance obstruction and
must not be read as one: distinct branched covers are exactly what one expects
for non-isotopic knots, and Abe–Tagami already proved K_n ≅ K_m only when
n = m or n + m = −1. What it does is make the branched-cover route concrete.

## 3. The gate this opens, stated exactly

Because Σ₂(K₀) is a **lens space**, one half of the d-invariant slice
obstruction is fully computable, and it is computed in
`scripts/lens_d_invariants.py` (controls: the published values for −L(2,1),
−L(3,1), −L(5,1) are reproduced).

    d(L(13,5)) = { 0, ±2/13, ±2/13, ±6/13, ±6/13, ±8/13, ±8/13 }

thirteen values, symmetric under negation — as it must be, since 6_3 is
negative amphichiral and L(13,5) ≅ −L(13,5) because 5 · 8 ≡ 1 (mod 13). So
no orientation convention for Σ₂(K₀) is load-bearing.

**Claim (elementary, offered for audit; not a new published result).**
Suppose D01 = K₀ # (−K₁) is smoothly slice. Then Σ₂(D01) = L(13,5) #
−Σ₂(K₁) bounds a rational homology 4-ball, so the d-invariants vanish on a
coset of a metabolizer M of the linking form on H₁ = Z/13 ⊕ Z/13. The linking
form is nondegenerate on each Z/13 factor, so M contains neither factor and,
having order 13, is the graph of an isomorphism Z/13 → Z/13; in particular M
projects onto both factors. Additivity of d under connected sum, with
d(−Y, s) = −d(Y, s), then gives

> **D01 smoothly slice ⟹ the thirteen d-invariants of Σ₂(K₁) are, as a
> multiset, exactly those of L(13,5) listed above.**

Every step is standard (Ozsváth–Szabó; Manolescu–Owens; Greene–Jabuka) but the
metabolizer/coset bookkeeping should be replayed by a second reader before
this is relied on.

The value of this reduction is that it is **finite, falsifiable, and small**:
it asks for thirteen rational numbers attached to one specific closed
3-manifold with a 21-tetrahedron triangulation. A mismatch would prove
[K₀] ≠ [K₁] and close the primary lane of this campaign outright. Agreement
would be the first positive evidence the campaign has ever produced for the
concordance the lane needs, rather than another exhausted finite search.

## 4. The cheap route to the missing half is closed

If some diagram of K₁ had a definite Goeritz form, Σ₂(K₁) would bound a
definite 4-manifold and Ozsváth–Szabó's inequality would bound its
d-invariants directly, with equality under sharpness. It does not: forty
randomized diagrams of K₁ (rank-9 Goeritz forms) and of K₂ (rank-18) were all
indefinite, while 6_3 returns a negative definite rank-3 form with determinant
13 on the first try, as an alternating knot must. So the missing half needs a
genuine Heegaard Floer computation for a closed hyperbolic rational homology
sphere, not a lattice shortcut. No tool in the recorded environment does this.

## 5. Reproduction

```sh
P=../knot-venv/bin/python
$P scripts/branched_double_cover_gate.py results/branched_double_cover_gate.json
$P scripts/lens_d_invariants.py results/lens_d_invariants.json
```

Both scripts refuse to overwrite an existing output. Inputs are the committed
`data/knots/AbeTagami_K_*.json` PD codes; their determinant (13), Alexander
polynomial (1 − 3t + 5t² − 3t³ + t⁴) and exterior volumes (5.693021091,
9.120006501, 11.241489252) were re-verified here and agree with the values
recorded when those files were built.

## 6. What this does not do

It does not produce a slice disk, does not obstruct one, and does not change
the status of any candidate in `CANDIDATE_LEDGER.md`. D01 remains a CANDIDATE.
The separations in §2 are statements about homeomorphism type only. The gate
in §3 is stated as a conditional and is unresolved in both directions.
