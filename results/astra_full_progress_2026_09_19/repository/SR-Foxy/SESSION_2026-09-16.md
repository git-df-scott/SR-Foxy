## 1. The result: Miyazaki 5.5 cannot obstruct a Teichner sum

Five sites excluded the fibered ribbon partners `8_9`, `8_20`, `9_27` from the
Teichner lane a priori. The restatement they relied on dropped a hypothesis.
Miyazaki Thm 5.5 requires **every** prime fibered summand to be minimal in the
homotopy-ribbon order or to have no nonunit norm factor of `Δ`, and a nontrivial
ribbon `J` fails **both** — it sits strictly above the unknot in the ribbon order,
and Fox–Milnor makes `Δ_J` itself a nonunit norm.

So the theorem does not apply, and the general form is stronger than the
three-partner correction: **Miyazaki 5.5 can never obstruct `K # J` for any
nontrivial ribbon `J`**, which is exactly what the Teichner lane requires to be
true. The wrong claim was in tension with the repository's own reason for running
the lane.

The correct rule, which is not the negation of the wrong one: the sum is excluded
**iff every prime summand of `J`** satisfies an alternative. That never excludes a
prime fibered ribbon `J`, and it *does* exclude `J = L # (-L)` — the square knot
being the smallest case, on which no run can ever succeed.

Verified against two independent LaTeX-source restatements (Abe–Tagami
arXiv:1502.01102 Appendix A; Hom–Park arXiv:2507.20455, whose proof establishes
minimality for every summand before invoking 5.5). The AMS original was **not**
obtained — `ams.org` returns a Cloudflare 403 — so [S07]'s evidence level is
**downgraded** from `PEER-REVIEWED PRIMARY` to `SECONDARY RESTATEMENT ×2`; the
earlier "checked through the original" cannot be substantiated from this clone.

Machine-checked: `scripts/miyazaki_hypothesis_check.py`, exhaustive over `Z`, no
floating point. All five ribbon partners FAIL the norm condition with explicit
witnesses; controls `3_1`, `4_1` and `K_n` PASS. `K_n` passing is why the
non-ribbon certificate on `D_{n,m}` is untouched.

**Running:** `D_{0,1} # J` for `J ∈ {8_9, 8_20, 9_27}` at 33, 33, 34 crossings,
pre-registered in `results/PREREG_teichner_fibered_partners_2026-09-16.md` in the

Closed a tempting shortcut for `research/22` §3.4(2): adding split unknots to
reach `n ≥ 3` **cannot** give Theorem 1 teeth. In Eisermann's convention the
split factor is `δ = -q - q^{-1}` with `δ(i) = 0`, so each split component adds
exactly 1 to `null V`; a 2-component slice link has `null V = 1` automatically,
so `L ⊔ O` has `n = 3` and `null V = 2 = n-1`, automatic again. Control run today,
`controls_pass = True`: `null V(O²) = 1`, `null V(O³) = 2`, `null V(O⁴) = 3`,
`det V = 1` throughout — which is what pins the convention. §3.4(2) therefore
needs a **non-split** 3-component slice link, and a literature search produced
none whose ribbonness is open. **I do not know of such a link.** Reported as a
null.
